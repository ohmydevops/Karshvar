About Karshvar

I built Karshvar to make discovering the world easier for people who love reading history. My biggest difficulty was always geography: from Anatolia to the Caucasus, from the Iranian plateau to Byzantium, I kept losing track of where important events happened.

So I started studying geography, and this game grew out of that learning. It is both a way to test myself and a step toward the offline companion I wanted for exploring the world. I plan to add more historical features over time.

Karshvar takes its name from an ancient Iranian word related to today’s Persian word for country. In Avestan cosmology, the world was described as seven regions.

Iran: Five historic places to discover

Persepolis · Pasargadae · Chogha Zanbil · Susa · Shahr-i Sokhta

Englishفارسی
1

Persepolis

The ceremonial terraces of the Achaemenid kings make a striking introduction to ancient Iran. Slow down at the stairways: the carved delegations and their clothing tell a more intimate story than the scale of the palace alone.

Read the UNESCO entry
2

Pasargadae

Cyrus the Great’s capital feels very different from Persepolis. Its scattered remains sit in an open plain. Look at the compact tomb of Cyrus, then consider how the palace and garden remains once formed a royal landscape.

Read the UNESCO entry
3

Chogha Zanbil

This Elamite sacred city predates the Persian Empire. Its great brick ziggurat is the focal point, but the enclosing walls help you imagine the wider religious complex. Notice how layers of brick give the monument its rhythm.

Read the UNESCO entry
4

Susa

Susa is a place to think in layers. Successive communities built here for thousands of years, leaving traces of homes, administration and palaces. The archaeological mounds reward an interest in how cities change, rather than a search for one complete building.

Read the UNESCO entry
5

Shahr-i Sokhta

Away from the royal capitals, this Bronze Age settlement reveals another side of early urban life. Its residential areas, workshops and burial grounds point to a society connected by craft and trade. Think of a working city, not simply a collection of ruins.

Read the UNESCO entry
