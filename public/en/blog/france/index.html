About Karshvar

I built Karshvar to make discovering the world easier for people who love reading history. My biggest difficulty was always geography: from Anatolia to the Caucasus, from the Iranian plateau to Byzantium, I kept losing track of where important events happened.

So I started studying geography, and this game grew out of that learning. It is both a way to test myself and a step toward the offline companion I wanted for exploring the world. I plan to add more historical features over time.

Karshvar takes its name from an ancient Iranian word related to today’s Persian word for country. In Avestan cosmology, the world was described as seven regions.

France: Five historic places to discover

Pont du Gard · Arles · Orange · Maison Carrée, Nîmes · Vézère Valley

EnglishFrançais
1

Pont du Gard

This Roman aqueduct bridge is an engineering lesson you can read from the riverbank. Its stacked arches carried water across a difficult landscape. Look at the rhythm of the stonework and imagine the longer water supply system of which this was only one part.

Read the UNESCO entry
2

Arles

Arles is useful for seeing how a modern town lives around older monuments. Its Roman amphitheatre and theatre are major landmarks, while the Romanesque heritage adds another period to the story. Do not mistake every historic building here for a Roman one.

Read the UNESCO entry
3

Orange

The Roman theatre at Orange is memorable for its surviving stage wall. Think about how architecture framed a performance for a large audience. The triumphal arch adds another perspective on the public monuments of Roman Gaul.

Read the UNESCO entry
4

Maison Carrée, Nîmes

This Roman temple is a good place to slow down and look at proportion. Its columns, raised podium and façade make a compact lesson in classical architecture. Unlike a sprawling archaeological park, the interest is concentrated in one remarkably legible building.

Read the UNESCO entry
5

Vézère Valley

The Vézère Valley takes you much further back, into prehistory. Its caves and archaeological sites reveal a long relationship between people and landscape. Original decorated caves have conservation restrictions; distinguish an original site from a visitor replica when planning.

Read the UNESCO entry
